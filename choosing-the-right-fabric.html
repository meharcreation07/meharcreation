Choosing the Right Fabric

With dozens of fabric names floating around — Organza, Georgette, Nazneen, Chiffon, Santoon — it's easy to get stuck. You don't need to memorize every fabric type. You just need to answer a few simple questions, and the right choice usually becomes obvious.

Start with the purpose, not the name

Before asking "which fabric," ask "what is it for?" A dupatta, a lining, a flowing gown, and a structured blouse each need very different things from a fabric — even if they all end up in the same outfit.

If you're making…Look for a fabric that's…
A flowing dupatta or sareeLight, sheer, good drape — Organza, Nazneen, Chiffon
Lining for a lehenga or gownSoft, breathable, low sheen — Santoon, Viscose
A structured blouse or jacketFirmer weave, more body — Taffeta, Chanderi
Festive or designer wearTexture and shine — Sequence Nazneen, Bright Pattern fabrics

Then think about season and comfort

Synthetic fabrics like nylon and polyester hold color well and are budget-friendly, but they breathe less. Viscose and cotton-based fabrics work better in heat and for pieces worn close to the skin. If it's festive wear worn for a few hours, breathability matters less. If it's daily wear, it matters a lot.

Decide RFD or already dyed

If you have an exact shade in mind — say, matching a specific bridal color — go with an RFD (Ready For Dyeing) fabric and get it dyed precisely to that shade. If a ready color already works for your order, buying pre-dyed saves you a step and a bit of cost.

When in doubt, ask

Even experienced designers double-check with their supplier before a big order — a fabric that looks right in a photo can behave differently once cut and stitched. That's the fastest way to avoid an expensive mistake.
